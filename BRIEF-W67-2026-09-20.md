## HARD FACTS A NEW WINDOW WILL GET WRONG
- **MID-STREAM TTS WORKS. THE PROOF IS THE IDLE GAP.** In the 09:21 turn,
  TTS DONE 09:21:56.868 was followed by TTS START 09:21:59.387 - a 2.5s gap
  with the pump empty, while other gaps in the SAME turn were 13-22ms
  (00.421->00.434, 02.052->02.074). A queue filled once at end-of-turn drains
  back-to-back and CANNOT go idle mid-reply. Unit sizes say the same thing:
  89, 19, 111, 83, 36, 62, 236, 49. Greedy packing to 350 cannot produce a
  19-char unit followed by a 111-char unit. Those are separate `enqueue()`
  calls. `add_midstream_tts.py` IS NOT NEEDED AND MUST NOT BE APPLIED.
- **`KEEPALIVE_GAIN` MUST STAY NON-ZERO.** `0.0` is digital silence. Once W66
  bound the AudioContext to an explicit endpoint via `setSinkId`, digital
  silence STOPPED holding that endpoint awake - the driver idles and swallows
  the head of the first sound. `0.0001` is inaudible and keeps it open.
  Setting it back to zero re-breaks the first word. The remedy was already
  written in the file's own comment; it was the author's resource, used.
- **THE FIRST-SEGMENT CLAUSE RELEASE IS NOT A STYLE CHOICE.** Reverting
  ChatArea to sentence-only matching (`/^[\s\S]*[.!?](?=\s)/` for the first
  segment) re-adds ~3.4s before synthesis is even asked. It will present as a
  latency problem. It is a GATING problem.
- **A CLAUSE RELEASE CAN EMIT A TINY FIRST UNIT.** Seen: 8 chars. The 20-char
  floor applies to the SEGMENT released; ttsPlayer then breaks it at commas,
  so a leading "Of course," becomes its own unit and still pays the ~1.0s
  per-request floor. Accepted trade. If a stutter after the first word is ever
  reported, this is the cause, not a bug.
- **F-W62-2d IS REGRESSION-VERIFIED ONLY.** The 30s timeout branch was NOT
  exercised - that needs a hung backend. Happy path confirmed unchanged.
- **HERE-STRING ANCHORS FAIL ON THIS REPO'S CRLF FILES.** A multi-line
